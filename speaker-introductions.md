## 1:05 PM — Serena Yeung-Levy (Stanford University)

> Our first keynote is **Serena Yeung-Levy**, Assistant Professor of Biomedical
> Data Science at Stanford University, with courtesy appointments in Computer
> Science and Electrical Engineering. She directs Stanford's Medical AI and
> Computer Vision Lab and is a Chan Zuckerberg Biohub San Francisco Investigator.
> Serena earned her PhD at Stanford under Fei-Fei Li, and was a Harvard TEAM
> postdoctoral fellow before joining the Stanford faculty. Her research bridges
> core computer vision — action recognition, video understanding, and
> vision-language modeling — with real-world impact in medicine and biology.
> She's a pioneer of AI-driven medical computer vision and "ambient intelligence"
> in healthcare, and her recent work builds "virtual cell" generative models that
> simulate how cells respond to genetic and drug perturbations. She also served as
> program chair of the inaugural NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track. Please join
> me in welcoming Serena Yeung-Levy, who will speak on **"Multimodal Generative
> Models Towards Generating Biomedical Insights."**

**Quick facts:** ~14,400 citations, h-index 45 · Best Paper, NeurIPS 2017 ML4Health
workshop · Google Research Scholar Award (2021) · CZ Biohub Investigator (2022) ·
served on the NIH Director's AI Working Group · co-taught Stanford CS231N with
Fei-Fei Li.
**Do NOT claim (unverified):** NSF CAREER, Sloan Fellowship, Forbes 30 Under 30,
any ActivityNet "best paper."

## 1:35 PM — Vincent Sitzmann (MIT EECS)

> Our next speaker is **Vincent Sitzmann**, a faculty member in MIT's Department of
> Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, where he holds the Jamieson Career
> Development Professorship and leads the Scene Representation Group at CSAIL.
> Vincent completed his PhD at Stanford under Gordon Wetzstein, followed by a
> postdoc at MIT with Josh Tenenbaum, Bill Freeman, and Frédo Durand, before
> joining the MIT faculty in 2022. He is a pioneer of implicit neural
> representations and neural rendering: his works SIREN and Scene Representation
> Networks — both NeurIPS oral papers — are foundational to the field, with Scene
> Representation Networks earning an Outstanding New Directions honorable mention.
> His papers have been cited well over sixteen thousand times. He's also a
> celebrated educator, recognized with MIT's 2024 EECS Outstanding Educator Award
> and the 2026 Junior Bose Award. Today his group is building "world models" —
> systems that learn to simulate and predict the physical world. Please welcome
> Vincent Sitzmann.

**Quick facts:** ~16,400+ citations · SIREN (NeurIPS 2020 oral), Scene
Representation Networks (NeurIPS 2019 oral + Outstanding New Directions HM), Light
Field Networks, pixelSplat (CVPR Best Paper Runner-Up) · MIT faculty since 2022.
**Do NOT claim (unverified):** NSF CAREER, Sloan Fellowship, SIREN "best paper"
(it was an oral, not a best-paper award), a specific h-index number.
**Note:** promotion to Associate Professor is effective July 1, 2026 — kept the
spoken text title-neutral ("faculty member") to stay accurate on the day.

## 2:05 PM — Trevor Darrell (UC Berkeley)

> It's my honor to introduce **Trevor Darrell**, Professor at UC Berkeley and
> founding co-director of Berkeley AI Research — BAIR. Trevor earned his PhD at
> MIT and has been a leader in computer vision for nearly three decades. He is,
> quite simply, one of the most cited researchers in all of AI — with over three
> hundred and thirty thousand citations and an h-index of 186. His lab built some
> of the foundational tools and methods of the deep learning era: the Caffe
> framework, R-CNN for object detection, and Fully Convolutional Networks for
> segmentation — work so influential that in 2024 alone it earned three separate
> Test-of-Time awards, from ICML, CVPR, and ACM Multimedia. Beyond his own
> research, he has mentored a generation of the field's leaders and co-founded
> numerous startups. Please join me in welcoming Trevor Darrell.

**Quick facts:** ~336,000 citations, h-index 186 · FCN (~63k cites), R-CNN
(~47k), Caffe (~19k), ConvNeXt (~14k) · 2024 triple Test-of-Time (ICML/DeCAF,
CVPR Longuet-Higgins/R-CNN, ACM SIGMM/Caffe) · ICCV Helmholtz Prize & PAMI Mark
Everingham Prize (2017) · advisees include Kristen Grauman, Kate Saenko,
Louis-Philippe Morency, and Yangqing Jia (creator of Caffe).
**Do NOT claim (unverified):** IEEE Fellow / ACM Fellow status, a specific count
of PhD students graduated.

## 4:00 PM — Hadar Averbuch-Elor (Cornell University)

> Our next keynote is **Hadar Averbuch-Elor**, Assistant Professor of Computer
> Science at Cornell University and Cornell Tech. Hadar earned her PhD at Tel Aviv
> University under Daniel Cohen-Or, and a bachelor's with honors from the Technion.
> Before joining Cornell, she did her postdoc at Cornell Tech with Noah Snavely and
> was a faculty member at Tel Aviv University. She's been recognized as an EECS
> Rising Star and as a Zuckerman and Eric-and-Wendy-Schmidt postdoctoral fellow.
> Her research sits at the intersection of computer vision, graphics, and language —
> combining pixels with natural language and 3D geometry. Her work ranges from
> "Bringing Portraits to Life," which made headlines in the BBC and New Scientist,
> to influential research on shape generation, neural 3D reconstruction, and
> text-to-image diffusion models. Please welcome Hadar Averbuch-Elor, who will speak
> on **"What Do Multimodal Foundation Models Learn Beyond What We Ask?"**

**Quick facts:** ~2,650 citations, h-index 22 · joined Cornell Tech Fall 2024 ·
EECS Rising Star (2020) · Zuckerman & Schmidt postdoctoral fellow · Area Chair,
ECCV 2022 · notable papers: Learning Gradient Fields for Shape Generation (ECCV
2020 spotlight), Who's Waldo? (ICCV 2021 oral), Bringing Portraits to Life
(SIGGRAPH Asia 2017).
**Do NOT claim (unverified):** NSF CAREER, Google/Amazon faculty award, any
main-conference best paper/honorable mention, a "Snap" research role (her industry
roles were Amazon and Facebook).
